ANN BONNY — BIOGRAPHY

Ann Bonny (1697–?): a little-known pirate. A mixture of genres, transgression and refusal of norms; an elusive, shifting identity. Partner of John Rackham and, above all, ally of Mary Read, she mocked both her peers and the Royal Navy before disappearing from the records after Read’s death in 1721.

Ann Bonny (2020–2022–present): a shape-shifting trio from France’s Grand Est region. Noise rock, post-hardcore, experimentation and permanent tension. Born from the ashes of 37500 Yens and Rhume Carabiné. Still alive.

Directly following the short-lived Ma Secte — two shows, then gone — Ann Bonny is the outcome of years of crossed paths, shared projects and musical friendships. First came 37500 Yens, the duo formed by Franck and Jud. Then Harpon, with Franck moving from drums to guitar and Julie joining on guitar and vocals. Ma Secte followed, an ephemeral extension of 37500 Yens with added bass, second guitar and Julie. Finally, everything converged into Ann Bonny: Franck on drums, Jud on bass, and Julie on guitar, vocals and synths.

After two EPs — only the first of which received a physical cassette release — the trio takes another step with a first LP bringing both recordings together. It gives full scope to Cherry Jo Lee’s artwork and to the sound shaped by Sylvain Ma at Gnome Studio, who handled recording, mixing and mastering.

The band calls its music “blurry-noise.” In practice, Ann Bonny inhabits a space where the tension of Chat Pile meets the melodic breathing room of YC-CY, driven by uncertain rhythms, deliberate imbalance and everything crooked and unsettling.

The music is dark, organic and suffocating. It moves in jolts, compresses the room and sometimes makes breathing difficult. Yet through the mass comes Julie’s voice: soft, fragile and almost suspended. The contrast never calms the storm; it makes it more disturbing.

On September 25, 2026, Ann Bonny releases its LP on Bruit Sourd while Astero, the album by 37500 Yens, receives its first vinyl edition on the same day. Two records separated by almost twenty years, connected by the same urge to push noise, tension and emotion beyond their limits.
